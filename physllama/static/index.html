Quick guide

  1. Connect your sensor

    Turn on a supported Bluetooth device. Search, then connect one of the recognized devices. Close other apps connected to it. Match the printed device ID to the scan result before connecting. You can rename a device while stopped; its hardware identity stays visible.

  2. Choose what to measure

    Use each graph’s Sensors dropdown to choose channels, then set each device’s sampling interval on its card in the Devices panel (see “How sampling works” below). Start records all supported compatible channels on every connected device, even with no graph selections. Graph checkboxes choose what to plot.

  3. Plot your data

    Start collection. Each Start begins a new run, numbered 1, 2, 3…, and Stop ends it. Time is measured in seconds from the first Start of the experiment and keeps counting while collection is stopped, so runs are separated on the time axis by the time you were stopped. Dotted lines on the graphs mark where each run begins, hovering shows a sample’s run, and the CSV has a run column. Reset removes all runs and restarts time at 0. Use each graph’s “Sensors” dropdown to overlay channels from one or more devices. Stop collection before adding new sensors to record. Different units get separate labeled axes. Add graphs to compare views.

  4. Inspect and export

    Each plotted point is one recorded sample. Stop, zoom, and hover for values. Each graph’s Download CSV button exports that graph’s selected sensors from all runs, including samples outside the zoomed view. Export CSV at the top exports every recorded channel from all runs, including channels hidden from graphs.

    Save experiment writes a .physllama file with the experiment name, devices, their channels and sampling intervals, your graphs and every recorded sample. Open experiment loads it so you can continue: new runs are added after the saved ones. Saved devices reconnect with their settings when you search and connect them.

    CSV has a run column, a time column and one column per sensor. Each row is one time at which at least one channel recorded a sample. Every cell is a raw measurement taken at exactly that time; nothing is interpolated. Channels on the same device share sample times and fill the same rows. When devices sample at different times, each device’s cells are filled only on its own rows and are blank on the others.

How sampling works. Each device has its own sampling interval, set on its card; the rate in Hz is 1000 ÷ interval. All channels on one device share its interval. Channels slower than the interval are left out unless you plot them, so a slow channel you aren’t plotting never limits a fast one; a plotted slow channel limits the whole device. A channel that is left out is not recorded at all: it has no data in that run, in the graphs or in either CSV. The app warns when an interval change leaves a channel out. The card shows the device’s maximum rate and, when channels differ, each channel’s maximum. How the samples are taken depends on the device:
  • Go Direct — the interval is sent to the sensor, which samples on its own clock and streams the buffered values over Bluetooth. Sample times are the run’s start time plus a multiple of the interval, so the spacing is exactly uniform.
  • PASCO — this computer requests a reading from the sensor once per interval (polling) and timestamps it midway between the request and the reply. Polling is limited to at most 50 Hz, and Bluetooth round trips often make the achieved rate lower and slightly irregular, especially with several sensor groups. Each PASCO device card shows the rate achieved in the latest run.
  • Demo — simulated values at exactly the requested interval.
Devices are not hardware synchronized, so sample times from different devices do not line up. The CSV keeps each device’s samples at their own times, as described above.
Units come from the device library. Smart Cart position starts at zero each run; its velocity and speed are derived from the last three position samples.

Data lives in server memory. Refreshing this page keeps it; stopping the app or resetting clears it. All browsers connected to this service share acquisition controls and data. Export before closing the app. Demo data is clearly labeled and cannot be mixed with real measurements.

Open a saved experiment?

This replaces the current experiment’s data, name and graphs. Save the current experiment first if you want to keep it. Connected devices stay connected; saved devices that are not connected are restored when you connect them.

Clear this experiment’s data?

All recorded runs will be removed. Your devices and graph layout will stay connected. Export first if you want to keep the measurements.

Rename device

Leave blank to restore the advertised name. This name is saved on the computer running PhysLlama and shared by its connected browsers. The device’s IDs stay visible.

End this group’s session?

Export your measurements first. This stops your group’s recording, releases its devices and clears its data for all teammates. Other groups keep working.

Quit PhysLlama?

Export your data first. Quitting stops collection, disconnects your devices, and clears this session’s measurements.
